## Before we arrive

A little prep makes the day go smoothly.

- **We confirm the date.** Weather can move it; if it does, we'll tell you as early as we can.
- **Clear the driveway.** The dump trailer and the shingle delivery need to get close to the house. Move cars out of the garage and driveway the night before.
- **Take down fragile things.** Hammering shakes walls. Take pictures and mirrors off the walls on the top floor, and cover or move anything in the attic — dust and small debris come through the boards.
- **Kids and pets inside.** The yard is a work zone for the day.
- **Tell us about the yard.** Anything delicate, buried or easy to miss: prized plants, sprinkler heads, outdoor lights, a koi pond. Mark sprinkler heads with flags if you can.
- **Dishes and solar.** If you have a satellite dish or anything mounted on the roof, we'll tell you ahead of time what has to come off and who handles it.

## On the day

1. **The crew arrives and the lead introduces themselves.** You'll know who's in charge and how to reach them.
2. **Protection goes up first.** Tarps and plywood over landscaping, siding, AC units and the driveway.
3. **Tear-off.** The old shingles and underlayment come off down to the wood deck. This is the loudest part.
4. **Deck check.** We look at every board. If any are soft or rotted, we show you before we replace them.
5. **Dry-in.** Ice-and-water barrier at the eaves, valleys and penetrations, then underlayment over the rest.
6. **Flashing, drip edge and vents.** New metal at chimneys, walls and pipes, and the ventilation your attic needs.
7. **Shingles and ridge.** Starter course, field shingles, then the ridge cap. The roof takes shape fast once this starts.

Most roofs take a day or two. Bigger or more complicated roofs take longer, and we'll tell you before we start. You don't need to be home, but you're welcome to be.

## When we're done

- **A walkthrough with you.** We go over the job and answer questions.
- **Magnet sweep.** A rolling magnet across the lawn, beds, driveway and walkways for nails.
- **Gutters cleared.** Tear-off debris doesn't stay in your gutters.
- **Everything hauled away.** The trailer leaves with the old roof.
- **Warranty paperwork in writing.** The manufacturer warranty and our workmanship warranty, explained. See [how pricing and warranties work](/pricing-and-warranties/).
- **One number to call.** If anything ever comes up, you call the same person who quoted the job.

## Leaking right now?

If water is coming in, do these things in this order, from inside the house:

1. **Catch it.** Put a bucket or pan under the drip. Lay down towels or a tarp.
2. **Move what you can.** Furniture, rugs, electronics, anything under the leak.
3. **If the ceiling is bulging,** it's holding water. Put a bucket under the low point and poke a small hole in it with a screwdriver to let the water drain. It feels wrong, but a small hole beats a ceiling coming down.
4. **Take photos.** Of the leak, the ceiling and anything damaged. Your insurance company will want them.
5. **Stay off the roof.** Especially when it's wet, windy or dark. That's what we're for.
6. **Call us.** We do emergency repairs and temporary tarping. The first job is stopping the water. The permanent [roof repair](/roof-repair/) comes after, once things are dry and we can see what happened.

## After the storm

You can learn a lot from the ground. After a big wind or hail storm, look for shingles or pieces of shingle in the yard, tabs that are lifted or curled, dented metal vents, and a pile of granules at the bottom of the downspout. Any of those is a reason to get it looked at.

Be careful of anyone who knocks on the door after a storm offering a "free roof." Storm-chasers come and go. We're local, and a [free inspection](/free-roof-inspection/) with photos is the calm way to find out what, if anything, your roof needs.
