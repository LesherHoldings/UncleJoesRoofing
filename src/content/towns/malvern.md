## Malvern Borough roofs: King Street Victorians and old chimneys

Malvern took its name in 1873, when the Pennsylvania Railroad straightened its line and built a new station here, and the Victorian and early-1900s houses off King Street went up in the decades that followed. Paoli Battlefield Historical Park, site of the 1777 battle, sits inside the borough on Monument Avenue. Roofs this old are steep, so the water all runs to the same few places: dormers, valleys and old brick chimneys. Chimney flashing caulked over instead of cut into the mortar lets water in, and each freeze pries it open a little more. If the deck is still solid, a [roof leak repair at the chimney](/roof-repair/) is the honest fix. If the shingles are worn across the whole roof, we tear off to the deck, replace soft boards and rebuild the flashing as part of a [full asphalt shingle roof replacement](/roof-replacement/).

A Malvern address reaches well past the borough line. ZIP 19355 takes in the borough plus parts of East Whiteland, Willistown and Charlestown townships, all in Chester County. ## Wind across the Great Valley

The borough's older streets have mature trees. The newer neighborhoods out along Route 29 have younger trees and less shelter, so wind gets at those roofs more directly, and it lifts shingle tabs along the ridge and edges first. After a storm, [check the roof from the driveway](/what-to-expect/#after-the-storm) for lifted tabs, shingle pieces in the yard or bare underlayment. Shaded borough roofs have the opposite problem: moss and black algae on the north slopes after a humid summer.
